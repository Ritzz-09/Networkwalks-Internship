1. EXECUTIVE BRIEFING & PURPOSE
================================================================================
Networkwalks Security Services was formally engaged to perform an authorized,
black-box cybersecurity assessment of Mediroza General Hospital's public web presence
and patient portal (https://medirozahospital.com). Testing was conducted under strict
rules of engagement authorized by the Networkwalks Project Brief, prohibiting denial-
of-service, social engineering, or out-of-scope lateral movement.

The objective was to evaluate the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of
hospital assets across four defined operational milestones:
  - M1: Initial Access (Evaluating patient portal authentication & report retrieval)
  - M2: Cryptographic Analysis (Assessing PDF encryption & metadata forensics)
  - M3: Sensitive Data Exposure (Investigating database backups & HR intelligence)
  - M4: Professional Penetration Testing Deliverables & Strategic Remediation

================================================================================
2. KEY MILESTONE FINDINGS SUMMARY
================================================================================
- RECONNAISSANCE:
  External footprinting identified a LiteSpeed Web Server behind an HAProxy reverse
  proxy (ports 80 and 443). Direct HTTP requests to the web root returned HTTP 403,
  indicating surface-level access restrictions but leaving sub-applications exposed.

- MILESTONE M1 – INITIAL ACCESS (PATIENT PORTAL COMPROMISE):
  The patient login portal (/patient/login.php) was found to be vulnerable to classic
  SQL Injection in the username parameter. Submitting a SQL comment payload (' -- )
  completely bypassed authentication without valid credentials. The tester gained
  unauthorized access to the authenticated portal dashboard (/patient/portal.php) and
  successfully exfiltrated three confidential patient pathology reports.

- MILESTONE M2 – CRYPTOGRAPHIC DATA RECOVERY & FORENSIC METADATA LEAK:
  The exfiltrated patient reports were protected with Adobe Standard Security (128-bit
  RC4 / Revision 3), but utilized trivial user passwords. A dictionary attack utilizing
  John the Ripper and the standard rockyou.txt wordlist recovered all three passwords
  in under one second. Furthermore, ExifTool metadata inspection of the decrypted
  reports uncovered an unauthorized administrative comment by IT Admin "j.malik"
  revealing that a database backup had been relocated to "/old".

- MILESTONE M3 – CRITICAL DATABASE BACKUP EXPOSURE & HR DATA BREACH:
  Navigating to the disclosed directory (https://medirozahospital.com/old/mediroza_db_backup_2019.sql)
  confirmed that an unencrypted, 6.3 KB MySQL database dump ("mediroza_hr") was publicly
  accessible without authentication. Analysis of the SQL dump revealed:
    * Complete personnel records for 30 hospital employees, including National ID
      numbers, personal mobile numbers, email addresses, job titles, and salaries.
    * A total monthly payroll of ZAR 2,027,000 (Average monthly salary: ZAR 67,566.67;
      ranging from ZAR 19,000 to ZAR 160,000 across 17 hospital departments).
    * Complete shareholder equity records for 10 investors totaling 1,000,000 shares
      (88% Ordinary shares, 12% Preferential shares).
  [Note: The database backup dates from August 2019; while historical, the exposure
   constitutes an egregious breach of statutory data protection obligations].

4. REGULATORY & BUSINESS IMPACT (POPIA COMPLIANCE)
================================================================================
The vulnerabilities identified expose Mediroza General Hospital to catastrophic
regulatory, operational, and reputational liabilities under the Protection of
Personal Information Act (POPIA Act 4 of 2013) and international standards:
  - Compromise of Special Personal Information (Patient health / laboratory results)
  - Mass exposure of employee Personal Identifiable Information (PII) & National IDs
  - Unlawful disclosure of confidential executive remuneration and shareholder equity
  - Substantial exposure to statutory fines (up to ZAR 10 Million under POPIA Section 107)
    and class-action civil litigation by affected patients and staff.

5. TOP 3 IMMEDIATE REMEDIATION PRIORITIES
================================================================================
1. IMMEDIATE CONTAINMENT (PRIORITY 1 - IMMEDIATE):
   Immediately purge the "/old" directory and remove all database backups (.sql, .gz,
   .bak) from the web root. Implement web server rewrite rules blocking direct HTTP
   access to hidden directories and database extensions.

2. SECURE AUTHENTICATION RE-ENGINEERING (PRIORITY 1 - 24-48 HOURS):
   Refactor the patient portal authentication logic (/patient/login.php) to utilize
   parameterized SQL queries (Prepared Statements via PDO/MySQLi), completely
   eliminating SQL injection and authentication bypass attack vectors.

3. CRYPTOGRAPHIC & DOCUMENT SECURITY OVERHAUL (PRIORITY 2 - 1-2 WEEKS):
   Enforce AES-256 (Revision 5/6) encryption on all generated patient laboratory
   documents. Discontinue weak default passwords; mandate minimum 16-character
   cryptographically pseudo-random alphanumeric passphrases or authenticated in-portal
   decryption tokens. Implement pre-release metadata sanitization stripping internal
   developer comments from generated PDFs.
